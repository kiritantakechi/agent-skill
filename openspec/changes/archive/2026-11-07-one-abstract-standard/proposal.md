## Why

The four standards now share one form: one chapter template, one rule shape, one migration table,
one departures map. They do not yet share their *content*. Where two of them say the same thing, the
harness knows it only by accident, and three of the four relations between them are not written down
anywhere.

**Sameness is pairwise, anchored and hand-kept.** `Mirror` relates a standard to rust-style alone,
by rule title. zig-style holds 12 pairs; python-style and bevy-style hold none. A title-overlap
detector notes three more (PS-NAME-14 and RS-NAME-35, PS-NAME-15 and RS-MOD-43, BV-MIG-03 and
RS-MIG-02). Nothing relates python-style to zig-style. A concept all three state is three pairs at
best, and nothing says which of them is wrong when they disagree.

**The same concept lives in different places.** zig-style's `naming.md` covers naming, file and
module layout, declaration order, doc comments and formatting: five subjects. Rust and Python keep
doc comments in `documentation.md` and module layout in `modules.md`, and zig-style has neither.
rust-style's `cli.md` holds stdout, stderr and exit-code rules that python-style and zig-style keep
in `output.md`. The vocabulary fixes what a chapter is *called*. It does not fix what a chapter
*holds*, so a reader who knows where one standard keeps a concept still cannot find it in the next.

**The design principles are copied by hand.** Section 3 of python-style, rust-style and zig-style
asks five questions, four of them word for word the same in all three. zig-style's third differs,
and no record says whether that is the Zig domain or drift.

**Nothing tests what the standards do to a model.** Every check reads the text. None loads a skill
and asks whether a model given it triggers on the right files, cites the rule, or rewrites a removed
spelling. The descriptions have never been tested for triggering.

**The repository does not hold itself to its own assets.** The four shipped `ci.yml` files say every
tool is pinned exactly, yet all 48 `uses:` lines name a movable tag or branch of 8 actions, and no
standard states a rule on pinning actions. The repository has no CI of its own. OpenSpec's
instructions are delivered twice, as 6 skills and as 6 commands, generated by 1.15.0. The plugin is
still at version 1.0.0, although Phase C renumbered rules and removed every `CORE` ID.

## What Changes

- **One abstract standard.** A new document under `abstract/` holds every concept that two or more
  standards state, each a rule-shaped entry `AS-<FAMILY>-NN [tier] Title`: a language-neutral
  statement, then one table giving, for each base standard (python-style, rust-style, zig-style),
  the rule that realises it, or why its domain has none. A realisation is a departure-typed map,
  the type bevy-style's departures table already has: `realises`, `tightens`, `overrides`,
  `extends`, or `absent`, each with tier and reason. A delta realises a concept through its anchor,
  unless its departures table departs from the anchor's realising rule.
- **`Mirror` is removed, along with its pairs, its check and its controls.** **BREAKING** for the
  harness API. Tier agreement and look-alike detection move to the new `concepts` check. A pair of
  rules is now derived: two rules realise one concept. The detector compares every pair of base
  standards, not only rust-style with each of the others.
- **A concept lives in one chapter, in every standard.** A rule that realises a concept sits in the
  chapter whose vocabulary word the concept names. A standard's chapters are the chapters of the
  concepts it realises, plus domain chapters. Each domain chapter is declared in the profile with
  the domain feature that owns it (rust-style `ownership`: the borrow checker). `revise` gains a
  `move` operation, with laws, to relocate a rule between chapters. **BREAKING** for citations of
  every moved rule; the rename tables record each one.
- **A full sweep of the shared chapters.** Each concept stated by two or more base standards is
  registered. Each standard either realises it in the right chapter, departs from it with a reason,
  or states why its domain has none. Tier disagreements end as a tier change or a reasoned
  departure. A standard silent on a concept its domain has gains the rule.
- **Section 3 opens with the abstract standard's design questions**, in their order and wording,
  in every base standard. Each standard answers them in its own domain's terms and may add its own
  questions after them. A departure from a question states its reason.
- **Behavioural evals, generated and not run.** `agent-skill evals` writes a `claude plugin eval`
  suite under `plugins/style-standards/evals/`, built from the standards. For each standard:
  - a review case built from a verified BAD example, graded on whether the response cites its rule
    ID;
  - a migration case built from a `gone` row of the target release, graded on whether the response
    gives the target spelling and the owning rule;
  - a negative case in another standard's language, graded on whether the skill does not fire.
  Every grader is deterministic, so no judge model is called. A check holds the suite on disk equal
  to its generation. The owner runs it; this change does not.
- **Every action is pinned to a commit.** Each `uses:` in the four `ci.yml` assets names a full
  commit SHA, with its version as a comment. A build concept states the rule, and each base standard
  realises it. A check fails a movable reference.
- **Repository hygiene:**
  - OpenSpec is updated to 1.16.0, with skills as the one delivery and the duplicate commands
    removed.
  - The repository gets CI: python-style's own CI shape, plus a scheduled job running the full
    harness.
  - `plugin.json` moves to 2.0.0. **BREAKING** rule-ID changes since 1.0.0.
- **Harness debts carried from Phase C:**
  - `crossref`'s apparatus finding gains its line;
  - cache cleanup after a toolchain run takes cargo's lock;
  - Rust `env::*` rows get a probe;
  - controls run in parallel;
  - `install` exits non-zero when it leaves a differing directory alone.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `abstract-standard`: the abstract standard's form, the realisation map from it to each base
  standard and through the anchor to each delta, totality, tier agreement, the look-alike detector,
  and the design questions every section 3 opens with.
- `behavioural-evals`: the generated eval suite. It covers what each case is built from, what its
  graders check, determinism of grading, equality of the suite with its generation, and how the
  owner runs it.

### Modified Capabilities

- `standard-structure`:
  - Chapters are derived from the concepts a standard realises plus its declared domain chapters, and
    a realising rule sits in its concept's chapter. This modifies "Reference files are named from one
    vocabulary".
  - A revision can move a rule between chapters. This modifies "A renumbering is one operation that
    preserves every citation".
  - Section 3 opens with the abstract design questions. This modifies "Every SKILL.md follows one
    skeleton".
  - A shipped CI asset pins every action to a commit. This is a new requirement.
- `harness-integrity`:
  - The repository runs its own harness in CI under the shape python-style ships. This extends "The
    harness meets the standard it ships for its own language".
  - `Mirror` leaves "Per-skill facts live only in profiles", which now names the domain-chapter
    declaration in its place.
